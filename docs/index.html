Arabic Arena

A speech-to-text leaderboard scored on many test sets at once: MSA, Gulf, Egyptian, Levantine, Maghrebi, code-switching, noisy speech, and simulated 8 kHz telephone and call-center channels. One test set can mislead; the average of many is harder to game. Lower is better.

How scores are computed
  • Per set: corpus WER and CER, meaning total errors over total reference words or characters, after the same normalization is applied to references and hypotheses (Arabic: diacritics, alef/hamza/ta-marbuta spellings and punctuation are unified and digits are written out as words).
  • Per track: the unweighted mean over that track's sets.
  • Arena: .
  • Average tab: the plain mean of a model's Arabic and English Arena scores (both languages weigh the same), for the models on both boards; it follows the Scorer and Metric bars.
  • Scorer v2: per set, dialect-lenient normalization (fillers, elongation, same-word spelling variants such as برضو/برضه), space-insensitive alignment (ما فيش = مفيش), several references where a set has them (MGB-3's four annotators, Mixat's Arabic-script English) and every utterance's errors capped at its reference length. The Arena is the weighted geometric mean of the set scores; intervals come from a bootstrap that resamples whole speakers, videos or recordings, and the rank is a simultaneous 95 % confidence set. See the methodology page for the measurements behind each choice.
  • Uncertainty: 95% intervals from a paired bootstrap over utterances (1000 resamples per set, the same resample for every model). The rank range is how far a model's Arena rank moves across resamples; models whose ranges overlap are not reliably separated.
  • Telephony: v1 board: 8 kHz, a 300–3400 Hz band, a G.711, GSM, AMR-NB, G.726 or Opus codec, and packet loss. v2 board: chain v2, an A-law-first MENA codec mix with AMR-WB, Opus and G.722 paths, tandem coding to A-law and losses on the encoded frames. Call-center adds noise and a room at the caller before that channel (v2: a handset room and unintelligible babble).
  • Decoding: greedy, language forced to the suite language, batch inference on one NVIDIA A10 (24 GB).
  • Models: every model run locally is ranked, whether its weights are open, gated or private; closed APIs are listed as unranked references.
